Dead Internet Theory

Investigation · Internet culture
Bottom line: The theory argues that much of the internet is no longer driven by real people. Automation, bots, AI-generated material, engagement farming, and recommendation systems are real phenomena, but the broader claim that most meaningful internet activity is fake is not established.

What is claimed?

Supporters argue that the internet has become dominated by automated accounts and synthetic content, making it appear more populated and organic than it really is.

What is documented?

Bots, spam networks, automated publishing, recommendation algorithms, AI-generated content, and coordinated engagement are all documented parts of the modern web.

Where does the theory go further?

The stronger version claims that authentic human activity became a minority or that governments and corporations intentionally replaced much of the human web. Those broader claims require evidence that is generally not provided.

What evidence exists?

Researchers and platforms publish data about bots, automated traffic, spam, and synthetic media. These demonstrate significant automation without proving that the internet as a whole has become a fabricated environment.

Our assessment

Claim status: Partially grounded, broader conclusion unsupported. The ingredients of the theory are real; its sweeping conclusion is not demonstrated by those facts alone.
